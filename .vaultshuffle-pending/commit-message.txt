Ask what the sitting needs, declare what Steam can support, and tidy the surfaces

Session now asks about the sitting rather than the size of the commitment.
It was answered entirely from remaining playthrough length, so a fifty-hour
roguelike was barred from a short evening - a run is twenty minutes and
finishing one is a real ending - while a two-hour narrative game was Short's
best suggestion, when stopping halfway is the worst way to play it. Steam
tags separate the two well enough without modelling. A game leaning clearly
one way is eligible for a short sitting whatever its length, or kept out of
one however brief, and earns a few points either way inside the session
score, capped so it can never outrank fit. Tags that say nothing leave the
old length-based answer exactly as it was.

Steam's three gifts - the library, playtime, and when you last played - now
exist as declared capabilities rather than ad-hoc reads. Read ad-hoc, a
feature wanting progress reads a missing playtime as zero and one wanting
recency reads a missing date as "never", which is precisely how Purge came
to call every undated game untouched for nine years. Features state what
they need instead. Consumed for real, not decoratively: the play streak asks
for canUseHistory, and the Recently Played and Fallen Off shelves are no
longer offered to an account with no recency evidence, because they could
only ever come out empty.

Banners live on the Dashboard alone. Vault, Library and Purge are each for
doing one thing, and a strip asking you to go and do something else at the
top of them was in the way. The cap and the priority order moved across
with them.

Four smaller corrections. The value podium is one purple treatment rather
than gold, silver and bronze, which made a row of the same card look like
three different components when the ordinal already says which is which.
Library cards are a fixed height, with long titles and meta lines truncated
- a wrapping meta line, not the title, was what left a ragged row. The "est"
suffix is gone from the percentage, since the duration beside it already
says estimated. And the Vault's three actions are filled in the colour of
what they do with white text, matching the badges those states wear
elsewhere: green to play it, purple to put it away, amber to keep it to
hand.
